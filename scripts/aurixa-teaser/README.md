# The teaser's renderers

The teaser, _Three Questions_ (`docs/brands/aurixa-teaser/PLAN.md`), is drawn in code: the Earth's night side
from orbit, Sydney at night from above, and the lines, windows and type over them. These scripts drew its style
frames and storyboard, and then the film itself: `film.sh` below takes the style frames' renders to the delivery
master.

```
scripts/aurixa-teaser/style_frames.sh <cache dir> <render dir> <assets dir> <fonts dir>
```

It runs everything below in order. Give it a Python with numpy, scipy and Pillow (`PYTHON=...`), Node with
Playwright, and about 4 GB of memory. Keep the cache outside the work's folder: its packages are large, public,
and fetched again in a minute. The assets are the product's pieces the windows show and the supplied logo, listed under `frames.py` below, and the fonts
are the brand's Playfair Display and Inconsolata. The work _Aurixa Teaser_ keeps both in its `files/assets` and
`files/fonts`.

| Script                        | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `fetch.py`                    | Fetches the public map data from the package registries into the cache, as Reel 1's `satellite.py` does. It reads the one file wanted out of each package and runs none of their code. The packages are GLOBE's land mask, NASA's Blue Marble and Natural Earth's relief (public domain), GSHHG's full-resolution shorelines (LGPL) and GeoNames' places (CC BY 4.0)              |
| `places.py`                   | Reads Australia's places out of GeoNames' `cities.pbf` with our own protobuf reader, writing `data/au-places.json` (name, population, feature code, point)                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `orbit.py`                    | Beat 1: the Earth's night side from 900 km up, looking up the east coast. A ray to every pixel, the sphere it meets, the land and sea, the ground in moonlight, the airglow over the limb and the stars. Every place is a spread of lights in kilometres, projected through the same camera                                                                                       |
| `gshhs.py`                    | Reads GSHHG's shorelines out of basemap-data-hires' files                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `citydata.py`                 | Puts the Sydney basin on a plane in kilometres around 14 Wattle Grove: a view (rotation, scale, centre), the land's share of each pixel from the shoreline, and people per square kilometre from GeoNames                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `city.py`                     | Beats 2 to 6: Sydney at night from above. The coast is real. The roads are drawn from where people live: the boundaries between neighbouring suburbs bent into wandering roads, with major roads between the largest suburbs and a street grid in each suburb. No real street is traced or named                                                                                  |
| `offices.py`                  | How the three offices were chosen: real suburbs whose points make a near-equilateral triangle with the property inside (South Windsor, Edensor Park and Hornsby, 28 to 30 km a side)                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `frames.py`                   | The ten style frames as pages. The windows hold the product's own screens (`D.png`, the floor plan; `r2-pp-cmd-cover.png`, the Passport's cover, cropped below its maker's name so the brand stays back for the end; `r3-bc-card-empty.png`, the Borrowing Capacity card), and the logo is the supplied art (`logo-clean.png`, `logo-art.png`), placed and blended, never redrawn |
| `shoot.cjs`                   | Photographs each page at 1080 x 1920 once its fonts and images have settled                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `board.py`, `shoot_board.cjs` | The storyboard: each beat of `docs/brands/aurixa-teaser/script.json` with its frame, photographed whole                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

## The film

```
scripts/aurixa-teaser/film.sh <work files dir> <living logo take dir> <scratch dir>
```

| Script          | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `sound.py`      | The soundtrack: the synthwave score cut on its own bar lines under the picture, its drops on the first question and on the answer, and its beat of silence as the held breath; the motif and the hits as synth plucks, stabs and 808s on their frames; the brand voice's eight takes (Gabby, through `generate_voiceover`) on their beats, the score's mids ducked under them. Writes the stems, `mix.wav` and `cues.json`: where every spoken word falls |
| `score_plan.py` | The score's request to Eleven Music (`generate_music`): dark synthwave in the reels' _National Treasures_' key, at the picture's tempo, in clear sections to be cut on its bar lines                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `master.py`     | The mix to -14 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP, by gain and a look-ahead true-peak limiter at four times the sample rate that touches only the speech and bell transients. Measured with FFmpeg's EBU R128 meter                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `orbitcam.py`   | `orbit.py`'s camera as functions, so `film.py` can measure the orbit's picture about the pin (a kilometre east and north of it) through the same camera. `orbit.py` takes `padx` and `pady` to draw past the frame for the dive's roll                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `film.py`       | The film as one page: the orbit, the three city plates, the haze, the windows and type (the style frames' own styles), the supplied logo with its lettering cut per letter and keyed for the rise, and the data `film.js` moves them by                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `film.js`       | Every instant of the 37.5 seconds, from the time alone: one camera holds the city (the plates are the same streets at 2.5, 7 and 18 px a km, placed by scale alone), the orbit turned and sheared onto them as the camera falls, the words rising as they are heard (`cues.json`), the three lines landing on the motif's notes, the triangle standing up into the emblem on the hit. `window.setT(t)` draws t and resolves once its pictures decode      |
| `render.cjs`    | Draws every frame at twice its size in Chromium on four processes, each a run of frames, with motion blur from drawings spread over a 180-degree shutter (eight through the dive, one at a hold); keeps a drawing once two captures agree. Joins the runs into a lossless reference                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `encode.sh`     | The delivery master from the reference and the mastered sound: H.264 High, BT.709 4:2:0 with error-diffusion dither, AAC 256 kb/s                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `qc.py`         | The delivery master measured against its lossless reference: the encode against the picture it was given, the master against the reference and the format's own ceiling, loudness, flicker and black frames                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

## The client's reference

`reference/` measures the reference teaser the client sent on 27 October 2026 (`docs/brands/aurixa-teaser/REFERENCE.md`
records what it found). Give them a Python with numpy, scipy and soundfile; `ocr_ref.py` also needs RapidOCR.

| Script                  | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `reference/separate.py` | Cancels _National Treasures_ out of the reference bin by bin in the STFT, with a complex gain fitted over a sliding 0.3 s, so the track's EQ, level and codec are followed and the voice is left |
| `reference/phrases.py`  | The voice's phrases, its pace in syllables a second, its pitch, and how fast each phrase's end dies away (a dry voice falls about 20 dB in tens of milliseconds; a reverb holds it up)           |
| `reference/ntgain.py`   | The reference's gain on _National Treasures_ by band, every half second: how the track is set, ducked or equalised                                                                               |
| `reference/ocr_ref.py`  | Reads every word on screen, every Nth frame at twice its size                                                                                                                                    |
| `reference/specimg.py`  | Spectrogram rows of the separated voice, 5 s a row, to see where it speaks                                                                                                                       |

Every script that reads downloaded data reads it as data: the packages' code is never run, and Python runs with
`-I`.
