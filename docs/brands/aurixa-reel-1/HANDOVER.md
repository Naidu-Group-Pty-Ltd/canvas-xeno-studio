## 1.2 The formula

> **Measure the voice. Write the film as data on its clock. Build every screen from the product's own code and every
> graphic from the brand's own CSS. Animate it as code that answers "what is on screen at time t". Render it as a camera
> would: drawn at twice the size, every frame averaged over a 180-degree shutter. Pace it to the music without ever
> stretching a word. Mix it with the studio's own mixer. Measure everything before anyone sees it.**

```
 voiceover ──► measured words ──► brief.json (shots, beats, captions, on its clock) ◄── the client's notes
     │                                   │
     │          four repositories ──► brand stylesheet (every value with its file and line)
     │                                   │
     │            products' code ──► plates (every page, fictional record) ──► graphics kit
     │                                   │
     │                              GATE 1: style frames ──► GATE 2: animatic
     │                                   │
     │        music ──► beat and bar grid ──► pacing.json (pauses in the voice, edits in the music)
     │                                   │
     │                         scenes: state(t) for every shot
     │                                   │
     │        render.cjs: 2x, 8 drawings a frame over a 180° shutter ──► lossless reference + master
     │                                   │                                   (in stretches, joined)
     │                              GATE 3: the proof
     │                                   │
     └──► paced voiceover ──► soundtrack.ts (the studio's timeline and mixer) ──► -14 LUFS
                                         │
                       assemble.py ──► film_qc.py (measured) ──► the master, delivered as rendered
```

## 1.4 The method, step by step

Seventeen steps, in seven stages. Each names its script, its settings and what it hands on.

### Plan

**1. Measure the voice; never estimate it.** `timing.py` reads the measured words and segments and places each cut in the
pause before its cue's first word, 0.15 s ahead of the speech onset (0.1 s ahead of a word mid-phrase), on the 30 fps grid.
The pack's own cue times were proportional estimates and drifted from the recording by up to 2.6 s.

**2. Write the film as data.** `system_edition.py` writes `brief.json` and `BRIEF.md` together, so the page and the data
cannot disagree. A shot is: an id and block, where it starts (a caption, a block, a phrase or a beat, resolved to a time),
its palette, title, sources (plates and graphics), beats (time and action), camera, what it ends on, its on-screen text
exactly, and notes. `--check` checks every time; `--studio-sql` keeps the brief in the studio beside the pack (creative
brief 1ebd06fc). The brief also fixes how every screen behaves:

- One action per beat, and a result held at least 0.8 s before anything else moves.
- The product's own pointer at 1.6 times its size, on the products' emphasized ease. A press is the control's own pressed
  state plus a cyan focus ring that grows from 0 to 24 px over 320 ms.
- Typing at about 16 characters a second, with the product's own caret.
- Anything the voiceover names is at least 22 px tall on the final frame.
- A screen changes only because of an action the viewer sees.
- The frame is used in zones:

  | Zone     | Height          | Use                                                             |
  | -------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | Top      | 0 to 250 px     | clear for the platform's own interface                          |
  | Rail     | 250 to 360 px   | the journey rail                                                |
  | Action   | 360 to 1300 px  | the window, the action, the result                              |
  | Captions | 1300 to 1560 px | the captions                                                    |
  | Bottom   | 1560 to 1920 px | clear for the platform; nothing within 120 px of the right edge |

**3. Read the brand from its code.** The studio's brand-code scanner (`read_brand_code`, docs/brand-code-import.md) read
the four repositories together. `reel_css.ts` turned the result into `aurixa-reel.css`: 58 custom properties, each naming
the repository, file and line it is declared on. The site's `@theme` is the source the others name, so it wins where they
disagree, and every disagreement is written down in the brief. `reel_preview.cjs` draws the token sheet. Nothing is
picked by eye.

### Build

**4. Build every screen from the product's code.** `plates.cjs` and `portals.cjs` typeset each page from the products'
components, labels and states: 41 plates, then 22 more for the six pages no screenshot covered (plus the third cut's
report charts, trial setup and marketplace). Three rules:

- **Each surface is set in its own fonts.** The portals' Segoe UI is drawn in Selawik (`selawik.py`), their mono in
  Inconsolata, and their serif in Playfair Display Medium. The faces were chosen by measuring them against the
  screenshots' own words: Selawik 0.208, against 0.264 for the face first used.
- **The report pages are the product's own PDF.** They are set line for line in Helvetica's metrics (`helvetica.cjs`), so
  they break their lines where the product's PDF code does.
- **Every record is fictional.** The property is 14 Wattle Grove, Banksia Rise NSW 2155 (a suburb that does not exist),
  graded A+ (84, STRONG BUY). Every Aurixa screen carries a Demo workspace label.

**5. Build the graphics kit once.** Twenty-nine graphics (`G-…` in the brief) in HTML and CSS on the brand's tokens, reused
by every scene. Among them:

- the glass window and the plain window, the pointer, the focus corners, the journey rail and the system map;
- the record card, the document, the floor plan and the wall of screens;
- the satellite globe and its pins;
- the stages in depth, the portal spotlight and the pages running past;
- the light sweep, the sunrise, the logo leap, the living call to action and the devices in sync.

They live in `kit.cjs`, `stage.cjs`, `scenes/workflowStage.cjs`, `desk.cjs`, `globe.cjs` and `pages/`.

**6. Gate 1: style frames.** `frames.cjs` draws one still per block at its key beat, under its caption, in the final look
(15 stills at 1080 x 1920). Nothing moves until they are approved.

**7. Gate 2: the animatic.** `animatic.cjs` lays the shots out as timed stills on the voiceover with the captions, cut on
the brief's beats at whole frames. Every one of its frames is checked against its schedule. The timing is approved before
any final render.

### Animate

**8. Write each shot as a state over time.** A scene (`scenes/*.cjs`, 26 of them for 27 shots) returns
`{ id, shots, palette, html, measure, state(t) }`. `state(t)` says what every element looks like at time t, from the
shot's beats in `brief.json`. It is a pure function, so the same brief always draws the same film, and any frame can be
drawn alone. `motion.cjs` holds the toolkit: the brand's own curves and durations (ease-move, ease-settle, motion-press,
from `aurixa-reel.css`), the window, the pointer and its press, the focus corners and the rail. A scene that is missing,
or cannot be built against the brief, is left out, so any range of the film can be drawn while the rest is being made.

**9. Captions in the brand's type.** `captions.cjs` sets every caption in Playfair Display Medium in the site's silver
headline gradient, with one phrase a line in its italic: silver before the brand arrives, gold after, with a hairline
under it and one pass of light. How the lines move and sit:

- **Words.** Each word rises into focus from a soft blur on its own spoken time, measured from the voiceover. A word
  enters over 0.42 s and a line leaves over 0.22 s.
- **Runs.** A pause longer than 0.8 s ends a run of lines.
- **Panel.** Behind the words is one panel that reshapes from line to line. It is today's white card before the brand
  arrives and the brand's navy glass after (near-opaque since the third cut).
- **Cuts.** No run crosses a cut where the palette changes.

**10. The logo with a video model, without letting it draw a letter.** `logo_motion.py` makes the model's input safe and
lays the exact art back over its output:

- **Clean plate.** One image edit removes the art's lettering, blended back only where the lettering was, so the model
  never redraws a letter.
- **Takes.** Two Kling 3.0 Pro image-to-video takes through the studio API: the reveal (A1, 5 s) and the living card
  (B1, 10 s).
- **Alignment.** Each take is aligned and colour-matched to the exact picture it must end on.
- **Overlay.** The supplied lettering and emblem are laid over the footage, so the letters are the supplied pixels.

**11. Pace the film to the music (the third cut).** `pacing.py` builds the music's grid, edits the music and holds the
voice open, and `pacing.cjs` carries every caption and scene time onto the film's clock.

The grid:

- The track is 128.99 BPM (one beat every 0.465141 s), and four beats make a bar.
- The drop is at 22.255 s in the file and 29.697 s in the film, so a film beat is `29.697108 + n × 0.465141` s.
- The film's landmarks are the drop (the brand arrives on it), the breakdown at bar 44 (111.56 s), the four statements
  of Block 14 on bars 46 to 49, the re-entry at bar 50 (122.73 s, the closing card) and the final downbeat at bar 54
  (130.17 s), plus a 1 s ring.

The music is edited in four pieces, each a whole number of bars from where it is in the file:

- the intro's last four bars play again (+16 beats);
- bars 33 to 36 play again (+16 beats; they are the bars most like what follows them);
- two bars of the breakdown are left out (-8 beats), so its two-bar phrases stay whole.

Each splice sits 20 ms ahead of its bar line, under a 30 ms crossfade.

The voice is never stretched. It is held open in its own silences by whole frames (1,470 samples at 44.1 kHz): 51 pauses
that add 23.833 s. Each pause has a length it should be near and limits either side, and their lengths are solved by
dynamic programming so the cuts land on bars and beats:

- the frames added between two cuts pay `0.012 × (frames − the frames they should be near)²`;
- each cut pays its beat cost: 0 on a bar, 0.25 on a beat, 0.6 near one and 1 off the grid (weighted 1 in the body,
  0.6 in the opening);
- each group's total is fixed so its anchor lands exactly: the opening on the brand's frame (868), the body on the
  breakdown. The close is placed by its key words' onsets;
- the frames chosen between two cuts are shared among their pauses in proportion to the lengths they should be near.

Every time in the scenes and captions then moves through `pacing.cjs`:

- a word, by the pauses that come at or before it;
- a cut, by those that come strictly before it.

### Sound

**12. Mix with the studio's own mixer.** `soundtrack.ts` lays the sound out as a studio edit's audio, in the Editor's own
timeline schema (`src/lib/edit/timeline.ts`).

The layout:

- the paced voiceover on its own channel;
- the music as seven items on Music & FX, each with its gain, its fades and how far it ducks under speech: the intro at
  0.6 ducking to 0.25, the groove at 0.5 ducking to 0.22, the breakdown at 0.45 and never ducked so it does not pump
  between the statements, and the re-entry at 0.6 ducking to 0.3, ringing out after the last downbeat;
- the faders: the picture muted, the voiceover and Music & FX at 1.

The app's own `buildRenderRequest` and the media worker's own code (`media-worker/src/sequence.js`) then mix it exactly
as a studio render would:

- speech is found with `silencedetect` (-38 dB, 0.35 s);
- the music falls 0.25 s before speech and rises 0.45 s after it, with regions closer than 1.2 s merged;
- the buses are summed;
- loudness is measured on the mix (it measured -20.2 LUFS) and met in one linear pass to -14 LUFS, true peak -1.5 dB.

Measured in the speech band (300 Hz to 4 kHz), the voice stands 10 to 19 dB above the music in every section. The
layout is recorded in `soundtrack.json`.

### Render

**13. Render as a camera would.** `render.cjs` draws every frame in Chromium (Playwright):

- **Drawing.** At twice the frame's size (2160 x 3840), eight times a frame, evenly over a 180-degree shutter centred on
  the frame: drawing j of frame f is taken at `(f + ((j + 0.5) / 8 − 0.5) × 0.5) / 30` s.
- **Reuse.** A drawing whose state has not changed reuses the one before, so a still frame costs one drawing and stays
  exactly as sharp.
- **Averaging.** Each drawing is scaled to 1080 x 1920 with Lanczos and the eight are averaged, then kept as a lossless
  RGB reference (FFV1).
- **Master.** The master is encoded from that reference: zimg to BT.709 limited range with error-diffusion dither, x264
  High at CRF 12 (preset slow, aq-mode 3), AAC 256 kb/s.
- **Baking.** A layer that would make the browser redraw a heavy picture on every drawing (the wall's blur) is baked to
  an image once.
- **Records.** `film.json` records every frame's shot and caption words.
- **Chromium flags.** Images are decoded before a frame is drawn (`--disable-checker-imaging`). Every compositor stage
  runs before capture, so a busy machine cannot capture an unpainted tile (`--run-all-compositor-stages-before-draw`).

**14. Gate 3: the proof.** One stretch (04.1 to 05.2, 19.4 s) rendered and measured before the rest. It was approved, and
its two requested changes (steps 9 and 10) went into every shot after it.

**15. Build in parallel, render in stretches.** The third cut was built by six workers at once (F1 to F6):

- **Ownership.** Each owned a run of shots in its own git worktree (the opening, the brand and portals, the report pages,
  the workflow shots, the sunrise to the closing card, the explore phase).
- **Integration.** Each was merged into one integration branch with `--no-ff`. The frames on both sides of every cut
  between two workers' shots were drawn and checked.
- **Commit early.** Four of the six stopped at their turn limit, and their commits are what survived.

The film is drawn in stretches, each a run of whole shots, as soon as its scenes are final. `assemble.py` checks that the
stretches tile the film frame for frame and joins their references losslessly. It encodes the master once, with the same
chain and the soundtrack under it, and merges their records. A change that touches a few frames costs those frames:
`replace_frames.py` draws them again and puts them in their places.

### Check

**16. Measure everything before anyone sees it.** `film_qc.py` decodes the master as a player would and measures it
against its own lossless reference:

- SSIM and PSNR, frame by frame;
- the share of edge contrast kept;
- CIEDE2000 on flat fields and on the brand's own hex colours;
- banding;
- every caption against the record of where it should be;
- every cut on its frame;
- the voiceover's sync against the picture.

The second cut measured SSIM 0.989 and PSNR 43.9 dB, with no frame below 39.2 dB, 99.7% of edge contrast kept, CIEDE2000
0.41 on average, every cut on its frame and the voiceover 0 ms from the picture. `review_sheet.py` draws a contact sheet
of every shot's beats for a person to look at.

### Deliver

**17. Deliver the master as rendered.** The studio's Editor re-encodes twice on export: an intermediate at CRF 14, then
the final at CRF 17, preset veryfast. The proof through it measured SSIM 0.988 and PSNR 42.6 dB, clean but measurably
softer. The renderer's master goes to the platforms directly.

## 1.6 The rules that kept it right

**Truth and privacy**

- **Real client data never appears.** The supplied screenshots are layouts only; every screen is the code-built page with
  the fictional record.
- **Nothing is fabricated as a result.** The twelve guardrails of the studio's Aurixa Systems brand kit (no fabricated
  returns, approvals, valuations or results; no automatic financial, legal or accounting decision) hold on every page. Anything modelled
  carries the product's own illustrative-example label.
- **The product's own rules hold.** The Investment Compass never carries the purchase's financial modelling; the
  Financial Analysis does. So 05.2 runs the Financial Analysis' pages and 08.2 the Compass's grade, area and risk pages.

**The brand, exactly**

- **Every value comes from the brand's code**, with the file and line it is declared on, never from the eye.
- **The logo is the supplied file.** A model moves it but never draws a letter.
- **Names are spelled exactly:** Aurixa Systems, AURIXA SYSTEMS, Aurixa Agent.

**Time**

- **The voice is never stretched.** Time is added only in its silences, in whole frames.
- **Every cut sits on the 30 fps grid**, and since the third cut on the music's grid too.
- **Captions are the script's words**, timed to the measured words and never crossing a cut between palettes.

**Quality**

- **The render is deterministic.** The same brief draws the same film, so any frame can be drawn again and spliced in.
- **Nothing ships unmeasured.** The master is measured against its lossless reference first.

**Rights**

- **A licence is the client's to confirm and is recorded:** the music (unconfirmed for public use) and GeoNames (a
  credit is owed).

## 1.7 What it cost

**Provider spend.**

- **The live-action edition** spent on GPT Image 2.5 (Marketing Studio Sunburst and its image edit), Cinema Studio 4.0
  and Soul 2 training. The studio recorded no price for these runs (`costUsd` is empty on every take in `takes.json`),
  so the Higgsfield console is the only record of what they cost. Nine clips and the 4K logo takes were refused for the
  provider's balance before anything was made.
- **The system edition** spent only on the logo: one image edit for its clean plate and two Kling 3.0 Pro takes.

**Time.**

- **The second cut** rendered in 2 h 05 m.
- **The third cut** takes about 3.5 hours of rendering on a 4-CPU machine with no GPU, where the satellite globe is
  WebGL drawn in software.
- **The third cut's build** took six parallel workers between one and two hours each, then integration and review.

## 1.9 Making the next reel this way

1. **Lock the voice.** Prepare the voiceover in the studio for its measured words and segments. Never cut to estimates.
2. **Write the brief as data**, with `system_edition.py` as the pattern: shots, beats, captions, sources and on-screen
   text, every time computed from the words, `--check` clean.
3. **Read the brand from its code** (`read_brand_code`, then a stylesheet like `aurixa-reel.css`) and decide every
   disagreement in writing.
4. **Build the screens from the product's code** with a fictional record, in the product's own fonts. Use screenshots
   for layout only.
5. **Build the graphics kit once**; every scene uses it.
6. **Gate 1, the style frames.** One per block, in the final look.
7. **Gate 2, the animatic.** Timed stills on the voice, with captions.
8. **Pace to the music** if there is a track: grid, structure, bar-level edits, pauses in the voice (`pacing.py`).
9. **Write the scenes** as `state(t)`, on the brief's beats and the brand's curves.
10. **Gate 3, a proof.** One stretch at full quality, measured.
11. **Build the rest in parallel** if it is large: one owner per run of shots, one integration branch, every join
    checked on both sides, commits early.
12. **Mix with the studio's mixer** (`soundtrack.ts`), and keep the layout in its record.
13. **Render in stretches**, assemble, measure (`film_qc.py`), look at every shot's sheet, patch frames where needed.
14. **Deliver the master as rendered**, with its credits and its licences confirmed.

## 2.2 The proposals

Each proposal says why (what Reel 1 hit), what to build, how (the code it touches), and its size: S is days, M is one to
two weeks, L is a month or more.

### A. Motion and rendering

**A1. Compositions: code-driven motion graphics in the Editor (L, highest value).**

- **Why.** Every frame of the system edition was a composition the Editor cannot hold. That is the gap between what the
  client approved and what the studio can make.
- **What.** A composition item on the picture track: an HTML/CSS scene with a `state(t)` contract (the one
  `scenes/*.cjs` already follows), its own duration, and parameters a person can change (the record, the text, the
  colours from a brand kit).
- **How.** Move `render.cjs` into the media worker as a task: Playwright in the worker image, the scene bundle stored
  beside the edit, `film.json` as its record. The Editor previews a composition from its stills. Start with the kit as
  templates (the window, the pointer and press, the rail, the record card, the document, the pages running past, the
  call to action) so a person can assemble a product reel without writing code.

**A2. Camera-quality rendering as settings (M).**

- **Why.** The difference between the proof and a screen recording is supersampling and motion blur.
- **What.** Quality settings on a composition's render:
  - draw scale (1x or 2x);
  - drawings a frame (1, 2, 4 or 8) over a shutter angle;
  - reuse of drawings whose state has not changed;
  - a lossless reference kept beside the master.
- **How.** The settings and maths are in `render.cjs`: the subframe times, the tmix average, the Lanczos scale and the
  zimg dither.

**A3. Render in stretches across machines, and cache by shot (M).**

- **Why.** The third cut took about 3.5 hours on one 4-CPU machine, and every change meant planning which stretch to
  draw again.
- **What.** A render split by shots across media-worker instances, joined losslessly (`assemble.py` is the pattern), with
  each shot cached under a hash of its scene, its data and its assets, so a change draws only the shots it touches.
- **How.** Show an estimate before starting: the drawings a frame times the measured cost a drawing.

**A4. A mastering export (S).**

- **Why.** Export re-encodes twice (CRF 14, then CRF 17 veryfast), which measurably softened the proof.
- **What.**
  - Pass through a finished master untouched when the edit is one clip with no changes over it.
  - Offer a mastering setting for the final encode: CRF 12, preset slow, aq-mode 3, from a lossless intermediate.
- **How.** `FINAL_ENCODING` and `intermediateEncoding` in `media-worker/src/sequence.js`.

**A5. GPU rendering for WebGL compositions (M).**

- **Why.** The satellite globe was WebGL drawn in software: 16 to 23 s a drawing at first, 0.6 s after a day of
  optimisation.
- **What.** A GPU-backed worker class for compositions that declare WebGL, and guidance (the globe's own techniques:
  plain textures, a quantised clock, resolution per layer) for the rest.

### B. Sound

**B1. Import audio through the API (S).**

- **Why.** The API's only import is `import_image`, so the paced voiceover and the music could not go into the library
  from a script.
- **What.** `import_audio`: WAV, MP3, M4A or FLAC from base64 or an https address, into the same library and the same
  licensed-upload record the app's upload (`audio-upload.ts`) writes.

**B2. Pace a voiceover in its silences (M, high value).**

- **Why.** The client's "give the video room to breathe" meant adding 23.8 s to a 106 s voice without moving a word. As
  edit items that is 52 audio items against a limit of 16.
- **What.** A voiceover item carries pauses: a list of (where, how many frames), inserted only in measured silences.
  Captions and title overlays move with it.
- **How.** The item's schema in `timeline.ts`, the plan in `sequence.js`, and the warp functions in `pacing.cjs`: a word
  moves by the pauses at or before it, a cut by those strictly before it.

**B3. Bars, downbeats and sections (M).**

- **Why.** Beats alone could not place the brand's arrival on the drop, Block 14's statements on the breakdown's bars,
  or the closing card on the re-entry.
- **What.** Extend `analyzeBeats` with:
  - the bar's phase (which beat is one);
  - downbeats;
  - section changes (the drop, the breakdown, the re-entry), from energy and novelty.

**B4. Edit music on its bars (M).**

- **Why.** Fitting the music to the film took three bar-level edits: four intro bars again, four groove bars again, and
  two breakdown bars out.
- **What.** Fit a track to a length by repeating or removing whole bars, choosing the bars most like their neighbours
  (pacing.py's similarity), with each splice 20 ms ahead of its bar line under a 30 ms crossfade.

**B5. One solver for cuts, voice and music (M).**

- **Why.** `snapCutsToBeats` moves one cut at a time by changing a clip's length, and cannot move time itself.
- **What.** The dynamic programme in `pacing.py` as an option:
  - each cut's beat cost (bar 0, beat 0.25, near 0.6, off 1);
  - a quadratic cost for moving a pause away from the length it should be near;
  - fixed anchors.

  It is solved over the whole edit, so the result is the best arrangement rather than a series of nearest moves.

**B6. Ducking by section, and a mix report (S).**

- **Why.** The breakdown had to be left unducked so it would not pump between four short statements, and the balance was
  measured by hand.
- **What.**
  - An item's duck can be off.
  - Every render reports the voice's margin over the music in the speech band, section by section (Reel 1: 10 to 19 dB),
    with the measured loudness.
  - The stems export with the master.

### C. Captions and type

**C1. Captions word by word, in the brand's type (M, high value).**

- **Why.** The client asked for the brand's own fonts, animated, and the Editor's captions are four styles timed by
  segment.
- **What.** A caption style that takes:
  - the brand kit's display face;
  - an emphasis phrase a line (italic, the brand's accent, a hairline, one pass of light);
  - each word's own time from the lip-sync preparation's measured words, rising into focus;
  - a panel whose material follows the picture's palette.
- **How.** `captions.cjs` is the reference implementation, including its scheduling (runs, holds, quick leaves) and the
  rule that a run never crosses a palette cut.

**C2. Captions that stay legible over anything (S).**

- **Why.** The glass panel read as a grey slab over white pages.
- **What.** Measure the luminance behind the panel and raise its opacity over light content, or offer a solid
  brand-colour panel.

**C3. More brand fonts in an edit (S).**

- **Why.** Four font files an edit (`LIMITS.fonts`) is too few for a brand with a display face, a body face, a mono and
  an italic.
- **What.** Raise the limit, or take a brand kit's faces by reference.

### D. From the brand's code to production

**D1. A production stylesheet from the Brand page (S).**

- **Why.** `reel_css.ts` was needed to turn `read_brand_code` into roles the graphics are drawn in (ground, surface,
  glass, ink, gold, eyebrow, ease-move …), each checked against the brief.
- **What.** On the Brand page, after a code import: map tokens to roles, check them, and download the stylesheet and its
  token sheet. The roles become part of the brand kit.

**D2. Motion in the brand kit (S).**

- **Why.** The brand's curves and durations (ease-move, ease-settle, motion-press, the 320 ms press) set how everything
  moves, and the kit has no place for them.
- **What.** Easing curves and durations as kit fields, read from code like the colours, used by compositions and title
  overlays.

**D3. Product pages as templates, with a demo record (L).**

- **Why.** Sixty-odd plates were typeset from the products' code, and the rename to 14 Wattle Grove touched every file
  that named the old record.
- **What.**
  - A library of product page templates (a portal's dashboard, a client file, a report page, a stock list).
  - One demo record per brand (the property, its address, its grade, the people) that every template reads.
  - A rename that is one edit.

### E. Privacy, rights and money

**E1. Redact what a reference screenshot must not show (M).**

- **Why.** The supplied screenshots carried real client names, addresses and bookmarks, and were usable only as
  layouts.
- **What.** On import as a reference:
  - find and blur names, emails, phone numbers and addresses;
  - mark the image as layout-only, so no generation or edit can place it in a frame.

**E2. A licence and credits ledger (S, needed before publishing).**

- **Why.** The music's licence is unconfirmed, and GeoNames' data is CC BY 4.0 and owes a credit. Both are recorded only
  in prose.
- **What.**
  - Each asset carries its source, its licence and its status (cleared, to confirm, not cleared).
  - An edit lists its credits.
  - Publishing or exporting for public use asks about anything not cleared.

**E3. Record every generation's cost, and check the balance first (S).**

- **Why.** `takes.json` holds 254 takes and not one price: metered GPT Image runs report none (an open roadmap item).
  Nine clips and the 4K logo takes were refused for the balance part way through a stage.
- **What.**
  - Record the price `estimate_generation` quoted when the provider returns none.
  - Show spend per project.
  - Price a whole stage against the balance before queueing any of it.

### F. Reviews and approvals

**F1. Review gates as part of a project (M).**

- **Why.** Reel 1 moved through style frames, an animatic, a proof and the film, each approved in chat.
- **What.** Gates on a project, each holding:
  - the artefact;
  - comments pinned to a frame or a time;
  - the person who approved it and when.

  Two versions can be compared side by side (the second cut against the third).

**F2. Notes to tasks (S).**

- **Why.** The client's 14 numbered notes became a plan by hand.
- **What.** Paste a note list; the studio maps each note to the shots and times it concerns, tracks each to done, and
  shows which version answers it.

### G. Quality

**G1. Measure every render (M, high value).**

- **Why.** `film_qc.py` measured what eyes miss: the double encode's softness, and a brand colour one level off. It also
  gave the client numbers instead of adjectives.
- **What.** Every render reports, against its lossless reference:
  - SSIM and PSNR;
  - edge contrast kept;
  - CIEDE2000 on the brand kit's colours;
  - banding;
  - captions against the script;
  - cuts on their frames;
  - audio sync and loudness.

  Thresholds turn a measurement into a pass or a flag.

**G2. Review sheets (S).**

- **Why.** Every shot was reviewed as a strip of its beats (`review_sheet.py`, `review_frames.py`).
- **What.** A sheet per render: every shot's beats half a second in, and its last frame, beside the brief's description
  of the beat.

### H. Planning from the voice

**H1. Storyboards timed from the measured voice (S).**

- **Why.** The pack's estimated times drifted by up to 2.6 s, and the fix was a rule: cut in the pause before the word.
- **What.** Storyboards compute cut points from the lip-sync preparation's words with that rule, show the music's beats
  and bars beside them, and accept a brief in `brief.json`'s shape (shots, beats, captions, sources, on-screen text).

### I. The agent

**I1. Parallel work by sections (M).**

- **Why.** The third cut was built by six workers in parallel. What made it work:
  - one owner per run of shots;
  - one integration branch;
  - the joins between owners checked on both sides;
  - commits as soon as a shot drew correctly.

  Four workers stopped at their turn limit mid-task.

- **What.** The Supercomputer agent can split a reel by sections, give each a brief with its neighbours' first and last
  frames, merge as each finishes, and check every join. Turn limits are set from the work, with a warning and a commit
  before the limit.

**I2. Render-aware scheduling (S).**

- **Why.** Workers' test renders and the final render competed for four CPUs.
- **What.** One render queue per machine, test renders at a lower priority, and the final queued so it starts the moment
  a stretch is final.

### J. Generation, from the live-action edition

**J1. A clean-plate recipe for logos and lettering (S).** The logo method as a studio recipe:

1. Take the lettering out of the art with one edit, blended back only where it was.
2. Animate the clean plate.
3. Align and colour-match the take to the exact frame it must end on.
4. Lay the supplied lettering back over.

No model ever draws the brand's letters.

**J2. Align a take to its end frame (S).** A take that must land on an exact picture: register it, colour-match it to that
picture, and cross to the picture before anything moves (`logo_motion.py ingest`).
