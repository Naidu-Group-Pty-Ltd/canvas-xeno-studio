# Aurixa Systems: brand DNA and content formulas

What the studio knows about Aurixa Systems as a brand, and the twelve content formulas written for it.
Researched on 19 October 2026. The data itself is in
[`aurixa-systems.json`](./aurixa-systems.json): the kit's `dna` and `formulas`, exactly as they are
loaded into the studio's Aurixa Systems kit (and read back unchanged by the app's own parsers).

How brand DNA and formulas work in general is in
[`docs/brand-dna-and-formulas.md`](../brand-dna-and-formulas.md).

## In short

- **What it is.** For buyers agents, wealth advisers, mortgage brokers and property advisory firms
  who need to run client work, modelling, reporting and AML/CTF steps in one governed platform instead
  of a dozen disconnected tools, Aurixa Systems is a governed AI operating system for Australian
  property, finance and advisory firms.
- **What kind of brand.** SaaS (with a service side: configured voice agents and onboarding), selling to
  businesses. The Ruler, coloured by the Sage: in control, structured and premium, and evidence first.
- **Its voice.** The calm, exact register of its own Compliance Passport and Builders pages, not the
  "dominance" register elsewhere on its site ("weaponizing data", "apex players"). Australian spelling.
- **Its guardrails.** Twelve rules every piece keeps, most of them because Aurixa sells into regulated
  work: security wording, illustrative figures, no compliance promises, no absolutes, AI described
  exactly and labelled. They come before anything else in what the AI reads.
- **Its formulas.** Twelve, each written after a proven formula in the library, keeping its structure,
  guardrails and sources and never claiming stronger evidence. Ten are grade B (official platform
  guidance or research behind the format); two are grade C (an established convention).
- **Its mix.** About half of what it publishes builds memory with people not yet buying, about a third
  helps a buying group evaluate, and the rest asks for the demo.
- **Still to confirm** before publishing: see [Before anything is published](#before-anything-is-published).

## The content system

### The mix

About 50% of pieces build memory with people not yet buying, about 30% help a buying group evaluate, and
about 20% ask for the demo. Most buyers are out of the market at any moment (Ehrenberg-Bass, 95:5) and
shortlist brands they already know; buyers, including the compliance and finance "hidden buyers",
educate themselves before talking to sales (Gartner, G2; Edelman and LinkedIn). The split is an
inference from those sources, not a measured optimum: adjust it with the measures below. The studio
shows business brands a balance of close to 50:50 brand-building to activation over time (Binet & Field
for the LinkedIn B2B Institute), which this split sits near once evaluation and the ask are counted
together.

| Stage                                       | Formulas                                                                                    | Pieces a month at the cadence set |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Awareness: be known before the need         | Signal, Founder's Desk, Myth vs Mechanism; When… always on in paid placements               | about 7, plus the When… spots     |
| Consideration: answers for the buying group | The Governance Brief, One Client. One Clear Journey., Workflow Teardown, What You Use Today | about 4                           |
| Conversion: the demo                        | Under the Hood, The Walkthrough, Every Call, Into Action, Publish Your Stock Once           | about 4                           |

That starting rhythm is about 46/28/26 in organic posts; the always-on When… spots add to the awareness
share. If conversion pieces outrun the ask they earn (demo bookings), move Under the Hood to monthly.

**A quarterly live briefing** (the library's webinar formula) fits the consideration layer once there is
an audience: educational events build an audience a brand owns (ON24 benchmarks for financial services).

### What to measure

- Demo bookings and priority-access applications, by formula and channel.

## The twelve formulas

Each is written after a proven formula in the library: the same beats doing the same jobs, showing and
saying what Aurixa would. Timings are at the ideal length; a ★ marks a beat that opens or closes the
piece (a hook or a call to action), which keeps about its seconds, at most a fifth of the piece, when
the piece is shorter or longer.
"(added)" marks a guardrail Aurixa's version adds to those of the formula it was written after.

### 1. Under the Hood (`aurixa-under-the-hood`)

One task in the real Command Centre, from start to finish on synthetic data, in under 30 seconds, then one call to action.

- **Written after:** Pain → product proof (`saas-demo-ad`, grade B). Its grade: B, guided.
- **For:** any of its audiences. **Purpose:** Activation, built for consideration and conversion.
- **Runs on:** LinkedIn, YouTube, Website and ads. **Fronted by:** the product alone, no faces.
- **Length:** 15–30 s, ideal 25 s. **How often:** Fortnightly, one module per episode.

| At 25 s | Beat                 | Its job                                     | Show                                                                                                                      | Say                                                          |
| ------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 0–2 s   | The task ★           | Name the task, with the brand               | The Aurixa emblem and the task as a title in Playfair Display, e.g. 'Borrowing capacity, three lender policies, one view' | On screen: the task in plain words; no voice needed          |
| 2–6 s   | Where they start     | Show where the professional starts          | The real Command Centre on synthetic data: the client record before the task                                              | Caption: what they have, e.g. a client's income and expenses |
| 6–20 s  | The task, end to end | Do the one task from start to finish        | The real interface, zooming to each step until the result appears; every figure carries the illustrative-example label    | Captions name each step in a few words                       |
| 20–23 s | The result           | Land the result without a performance claim | The finished output: a white-label brief, a scenario, a Passport milestone                                                | Caption: what is now ready                                   |
| 23–25 s | Call to action ★     | One next step                               | The lockup and one button: 'Book Your Free Demo' or 'Apply for priority access'                                           | Book Your Free Demo                                          |

**Rules:**

- One module per episode: Serviceability & Lending Matrix, 10-Year Cashflow & Reporting, Suburb Snapshots, Aurixa Voice, Compliance Passport, Builders Network.
- A screen recording of the current build on synthetic data; label anything pre-release.
- Captions carry it: it must work with the sound off.
- The brand in the first two seconds.

**Avoid:**

- A tour of every feature.
- Speed or accuracy claims ('in seconds', 'error-free') without evidence.

**Guardrails:** `real-demo`, `real-proof`, `real-testimony`, `illustrative-figures` (added), `pre-release` (added), `no-compliance-promises` (added), `substantiated-claims` (added).

**Why it works:** Short product videos hold viewers (Vidyard: 65% watch under-a-minute videos to the end) and LinkedIn advises short, captioned clips; the real product doing one job is the proof a pre-launch brand can offer before it has customer stories.

### 2. When… (buying moments) (`aurixa-when`)

One buying moment, its consequence in one image, the Aurixa layer that handles it, and the brand: short films that tie Aurixa to the moments buyers think of the category.

- **Written after:** Six-second bumper (`bumper`, grade B) and ABCD video ad (`abcd-ad`, grade B). Its grade: B, guided.
- **For:** any of its audiences. **Purpose:** Brand-building, built for awareness.
- **Runs on:** LinkedIn, YouTube, Website and ads. **Fronted by:** anyone.
- **Length:** 6–15 s, ideal 12 s. **How often:** Always on in paid placements, rotating 6 to 10 moments per audience.

| At 12 s | Beat            | Its job                                | Show                                                                                                              | Say                                                                                   |
| ------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–2 s   | When… ★         | Name the buying moment, with the brand | The Aurixa emblem small in a corner and the title 'When [buying moment]' in Playfair Display on deep navy         | On screen: the moment, e.g. 'When a new client signs, AML/CTF starts.'                |
| 2–8 s   | The consequence | Make the moment felt in one image      | One cinematic night-office image of the consequence: files at 9 pm, a phone ringing out, a price list going stale | One short line, no claims, e.g. 'Six steps. Four inboxes. One client waiting.'        |
| 8–11 s  | The layer       | Show the Aurixa layer that handles it  | The real Command Centre screen for that layer, on synthetic data, with its LYR label                              | The layer and what it does, e.g. 'Compliance Passport: one guided journey, recorded.' |
| 11–12 s | Brand ★         | Leave the brand in memory              | The Aurixa lockup and 'Scale smarter through unified intelligence.'                                               | Aurixa Systems                                                                        |

**Rules:**

- Rotate a fixed set of buying moments per audience (from the DNA) and keep the look identical every time.
- The brand in the first two seconds and at the end.
- On-screen text carries it: it must work with the sound off.
- One moment per film, never a list of features.

**Avoid:**

- Claims or numbers.
- Fear about penalties.

**Guardrails:** `real-demo`, `label-ai` (added), `pre-release` (added), `no-compliance-promises` (added), `substantiated-claims` (added).

**Why it works:** Most business buyers aren't in the market at any time, so brands grow by being tied to the situations in which buyers think of the category (Ehrenberg-Bass) and remembered when they are; short, captioned films with the brand up front suit the LinkedIn feed.

### 3. The Governance Brief (`aurixa-governance-brief`)

One dated rule or regulator question, what it says, what it means for a practice, what good looks like, and one thing to check this week, with the source on screen.

- **Written after:** Explainer (`explainer`, grade B). Its grade: B, guided.
- **For:** any of its audiences. **Purpose:** Brand-building, built for awareness and consideration.
- **Runs on:** LinkedIn, YouTube. **Fronted by:** the founder, the team, a presenter (person or avatar).
- **Length:** 45–90 s, ideal 75 s. **How often:** Fortnightly on LinkedIn, numbered episodes.

| At 75 s | Beat                 | Its job                                                          | Show                                                                                                                              | Say                                                                        |
| ------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–5 s   | The rule ★           | Name the rule or question, with its source and date              | A title card in Playfair Display: the rule, its document and date, e.g. 'AML/CTF tranche 2, in force since 1 July 2026 (AUSTRAC)' | The rule in one plain sentence                                             |
| 5–25 s  | What it says         | Say exactly what the rule says                                   | The presenter or founder at a night-office desk; key words on screen; the document's title                                        | What the regulator says, quoted or closely paraphrased, with the date      |
| 25–45 s | What it means        | What it means for a buyer's agency, advice practice or brokerage | A simple diagram of where it lands in the practice's day                                                                          | Who it applies to and when, in plain words                                 |
| 45–65 s | What good looks like | Show good practice, then where a governed platform helps         | The real Command Centre on synthetic data, e.g. the Compliance Passport's six steps                                               | What a well-run process looks like, and how Aurixa helps run and record it |
| 65–75 s | Check this week ★    | One action, and the source                                       | A source card with the document's link and 'General information, not legal advice'                                                | One thing to check this week, then 'The Governance Brief, episode [n]'     |

**Rules:**

- Number the episodes and keep the opening identical.
- Cite the document and its date on screen, and link it in the post.
- General information only, and say so on the source card.
- Pair every episode with a 5 to 8 page document post of the same points.
- Compliance review before publishing.

**Avoid:**

- Penalty figures or fear tactics.
- Saying Aurixa makes a firm compliant or is endorsed by a regulator.
- Advice to individual borrowers or investors.

**Guardrails:** `real-proof`, `no-compliance-promises` (added), `substantiated-claims` (added), `label-ai` (added).

**Why it works:** Buyers trust thought leadership more than marketing collateral, and the compliance and finance 'hidden buyers' in a deal read it (Edelman–LinkedIn); dated rules are what the audience is already working through: AML/CTF tranche 2 has applied since 1 July 2026, and ASIC has warned the AI governance gap is likely to widen.

### 4. One Client. One Clear Journey. (`aurixa-passport-journey`)

The AML/CTF journey as it is (forms, emails, follow-ups), then the same journey with the Compliance Passport in the real product, and what stays the firm's responsibility.

- **Written after:** Problem → agitate → solve (`pas`, grade B). Its grade: B, guided.
- **For:** Buyers agents. **Purpose:** Brand and activation, built for consideration and conversion.
- **Runs on:** LinkedIn, YouTube, Website and ads. **Fronted by:** the product alone, no faces.
- **Length:** 30–60 s, ideal 45 s. **How often:** Monthly through FY26/27, then quarterly.

| At 45 s | Beat                                 | Its job                                              | Show                                                                                                                            | Say                                                                   |
| ------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–4 s   | As it is ★                           | Show the journey as it is today                      | A desk of forms, emails, follow-ups and documents, as the Passport page lists them                                              | 'Forms. Emails. Follow-ups. More emails.'                             |
| 4–12 s  | What it costs                        | Show what that costs the client and the team, calmly | The client unsure what to do next; the team piecing answers together across systems                                             | Where the client is up to, and who acts next, gets lost               |
| 12–32 s | With the Passport                    | The same journey in the real product                 | The real Client Portal and Command Centre on synthetic data: identity, documents, screening, ownership, source of funds, review | 'Invite. Verify. Review. Approve. Passport.' with each step captioned |
| 32–40 s | The record                           | Show what is recorded and how it is shared           | The Passport record, 'Issued · Current', its milestones and controlled sharing                                                  | One record of verified steps, shared only where authorised            |
| 40–45 s | Responsibility, then the next step ★ | Be clear about responsibility, then one next step    | A card: 'Your business remains responsible for its AML/CTF obligations.' Then the lockup and 'Book Your Free Demo'              | Run and record your process in one place. Book a demo.                |

**Rules:**

- Calm and plain, like the Compliance Passport page: no fear and no penalty figures.
- Tranche 2 is in force (since 1 July 2026): never 'coming soon'.
- The Passport is Aurixa's record of completed steps, not an official certificate or an AUSTRAC document.
- Never say sharing a Passport lets another firm skip its own checks.

**Avoid:**

- 'Become compliant', 'AUSTRAC-ready' or 'audit-proof'.
- Real identity documents or client data.

**Guardrails:** `real-demo`, `real-proof`, `fair-comparison`, `no-compliance-promises` (added), `pre-release` (added).

**Why it works:** Since 1 July 2026 a buyer's agent's AML/CTF obligations start when a client signs, and AUSTRAC expects effort and progress through FY26/27; the same journey shown before and in the real product makes the change concrete without promising compliance.

## Before anything is published

These are open, and each one changes what content may say:

1. **Whose security certifications.** The compliance page attributes SOC 2 Type II controls and
   ISO 27001:2022 certification to the infrastructure Aurixa runs on. The kit words it that way. If
   Aurixa obtains its own SOC 2 report, change the proof line and guardrail 1 to the exact wording for
   its type and period, and never "certified".
2. **A named founder.** Founder's Desk needs a real, named founder on their own profile; the site quotes
   an unnamed "Founding Partner". Never stand in an AI presenter for them.
3. **Compliance Passport sharing, reviewed by a lawyer.** How sharing a Passport between professionals is
   described, under AUSTRAC's reliance rules. The Act's reliance provisions (s26F, s38) were not read in
   full, and AUSTRAC's guidance gives two different start dates for the new reliance rules.
4. **"Command Centre" or "Command Center".** The site uses both (Center four times, Centre twice). The
   kit uses the Australian spelling, in line with guardrail 12; confirm, then add it to the kit's
   "Spell exactly" terms.
5. **What is generally available.** Platform access is by waitlist, and the Builders Network says
   "Registration Open". Every formula that shows the product labels anything pre-release; a list of what
   is live keeps that label honest.
6. **Aurixa Voice making outbound calls.** The Do Not Call Register, telemarketing standards and consent
   to record calls were not researched; Every Call, Into Action shows inbound calls only until they are checked.
7. **Licensed data.** Signal uses public releases only (MFAA, ASIC, APRA, RBA, ABS, Cotality's public
   reports). Anything from a data licence needs that licence checked before it is republished.
8. **Which advertising rules apply to Aurixa's own ads.** Whether RG 234 or the credit rules reach a
   software vendor's advertising for borrowing-capacity features needs legal advice; the guardrails
   assume they might.
9. **Every external quote and figure, opened.** All external sources were read through search. Open each
   before quoting it in public content: first the Edelman and LinkedIn thought-leadership reports, ASIC
   REP 798 and RG 234, AUSTRAC's tranche 2 and reliance pages, the Vidyard benchmarks, and LinkedIn's
   video-ad guidance.
10. **Compliance review** before publishing any piece with financial figures, compliance or security
    claims, a creator, or an AI presenter (the research's process guardrail).
