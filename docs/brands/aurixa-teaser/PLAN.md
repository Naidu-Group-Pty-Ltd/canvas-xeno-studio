## The idea

Every property raises three questions, and three different professionals ask them:

- **"Is it right for them?"** The adviser's question.
- **"Are they who they say they are?"** The compliance question.
- **"Can they afford it?"** The broker's question.

The teaser asks them over a city at night. One property is a gold light. Each question draws a line from it to an
office across the city, where a window opens on a glimpse of the answer being worked out: a floor plan, a closed
Passport, a button nobody has pressed yet. Then the windows go dark and three lights burn far apart: "Until now,
each one was answered somewhere else."

That gap is the reason the system exists. The three lights reach for each other and close into a triangle: "We've
been building the answer." Silence. Then the triangle stands up off the map and becomes the Aurixa emblem. It's the
first time the brand appears, with its name and one line: "Built for the people who ask them." Everything falls
away except one light, and the last word: "Soon."

Why it intrigues:

- **It's made of questions.** Every property professional knows these three questions, and the teaser answers
  none of them. The viewer finishes each one in their own head.
- **It names a real gap.** "Until now, each one was answered somewhere else" is how the work is done today. It gives
  the system a reason to exist without a single claim about it.
- **It shows the answer's shape, not the answer.** The windows are glimpses, open a few seconds each, never long
  enough to read everything. The product itself is the reveal, and the teaser keeps it back.
- **The brand comes last.** No logo or name appears until the triangle becomes the emblem at 0:30, so the brand
  arrives as the answer to everything before it.
- **It ends on a promise.** "We've been building the answer" says the work is done. "Soon" says it's about to be
  shown. The last image is the light the teaser opened on.

## How it looks

The picture is the one you approved in the second version. The storyboard and the frames are drawn in code, as the
teaser itself will be, and production builds on the same renderers
([`scripts/aurixa-teaser/`](../../../scripts/aurixa-teaser/)).

- **The night.** The camera is 900 km up over Bass Strait, looking north-north-east up the coast. Every town of 1,000
  people or more is a spread of light sized by its population (GeoNames). The coast is GLOBE's land mask, the ground
  is NASA's Blue Marble in moonlight, and a thin line of airglow sits above the horizon.
- **The city.** Sydney at night from above. The coast and the harbour are the real ones (GSHHG), and the light comes
  from where people live (GeoNames). The streets are drawn, not traced, so none is real and none is named. The three
  offices stand at the points of three real suburbs that make a near-equilateral triangle around the property, and
  none is named on screen.
- **The windows.** Each window is a glimpse in glass with a gold hairline, open about three seconds. Each shows one
  of the product's own screens on its demo record, labelled DEMO WORKSPACE: 14 Wattle Grove's floor plan in gold,
  the closed Compliance Passport, and the Borrowing Capacity card with the pointer waiting. The Passport is cropped
  below its maker's name but never altered, so the brand stays hidden until 0:30. Nothing is pressed and no figure
  appears.
- **The gap.** The city dims and the three lights burn apart, their lines to the property broken into dashes. It's
  the one moment the picture holds still.
- **The answer.** As the triangle stands up, the city tips back into perspective and its lights meet the emblem's
  own skyline. The triangle settles onto the emblem's edges and dissolves into the supplied art. The emblem itself is
  never drawn: the lines are the questions' lines, and the logo is the supplied file.
- **Soon.** The emblem fades almost to nothing and leaves its orb lit, the gold light from the first frame.
- **The type.** Playfair Display, with each line's key phrase in gold italic over a hairline, and the gold mono for
  labels. It sits on the night with a soft shade behind it, like a title sequence.
- **The cover.** Frame 0 is the thumbnail: the night, the gold light, and "Every property comes with three
  questions."

## The rules it keeps

- **No figures.** The Borrowing Capacity card shows no capacity: the pointer waits, and nothing is calculated
  (guardrail 2).
- **The product on its demo record.** Jordan Ellis and 14 Wattle Grove, with DEMO WORKSPACE on the product's screens
  (guardrail 8).
- **The Passport, but not its sharing.** It's shown closed and never passed between businesses, so it says nothing
  about relying on another firm's checks (guardrail 4). "Compliant" appears nowhere, and the Passport page that
  carries the word is left out.
- **A gap, not a promise.** "Until now, each one was answered somewhere else" describes how the work is done today,
  and "We've been building the answer" says only that it has been built. There are no absolutes, no performance
  claims and nothing about availability (guardrails 3, 5 and 9).
- **Spoken to the professional.** These are the questions a professional asks about a client, labelled with who asks
  them (guardrail 6).
- **No AI imagery beyond the supplied logo art.** The night views are drawn from data, and the floor plan is a
  drawing (guardrail 10).
- **The supplied logo**, placed and blended, never redrawn. The lettering is the supplied lettering.
- **Clear of the platforms.** The top 250 px and the bottom 360 px carry nothing that matters, and the lines sit
  between 1300 and 1560 px.

## Decisions

1. **The script as written.** Approve it, or tell me what to change. Once it's approved, it's ready to generate.
2. **The music.** _Recommended:_ the original score, which gives the teaser its own sound and needs no licence
   beyond your ElevenLabs plan. The alternative is _National Treasures_, whose licence is still to confirm.
3. **The voice.** _Recommended:_ the eight lines above, recorded in the brand voice. The alternative is no voice:
   the type and the score carry it, and it plays the same with the sound off.
4. **The last word.** "Soon." works with any launch plan. If you have a date for the reveal, it can replace "Soon."
   or sit under it in the gold mono, for example "THE REVEAL · 14.11.26". A date is the stronger hook, but only if
   it holds.
5. **Who asks.** The labels above the questions name the professions (the adviser, compliance, the broker), which
   tells professionals the system was built for them. The alternative is a plainer "QUESTION ONE", "TWO" and
   "THREE".
6. **A 15-second cut.** Optional, once this one is approved: the night (3 s), the three questions (2 s each), the
   triangle becoming the emblem (3 s), and the name and "Soon." (3 s).

## Before it runs publicly

- **The map's credits**, in the post's description: GeoNames (CC BY 4.0) and the GSHHG shorelines (Wessel and Smith,
  LGPL). NASA's Blue Marble, Natural Earth and NOAA's GLOBE land mask are public domain, and crediting them is
  courteous.
- **ElevenLabs' plan**, which must cover commercial use, for the voice and the score.
- **The compliance review**, if you want the teaser held to it. It makes no claim and shows no figure, but it names
  the Compliance Passport and shows the Borrowing Capacity card.

## How it will be made, once approved

- **The night views** are rendered in code at every step of the camera's path. The orbit falls toward Sydney through
  matched scales, and the city resolves beneath it (`orbit.py`, `city.py`).
- **The lines, windows and type** are drawn in the browser. The triangle stands up as a 3D move over the city tipped
  into perspective, and the logo is the supplied art, dissolved in.
- **The voice** is your recording, timed word by word. The score is generated to the plan above and fitted to
  37.5 s, and the mix is measured.
- **Two gates** in the studio project _Aurixa Teaser_: the animatic (the whole cut with its sound), then the film.
