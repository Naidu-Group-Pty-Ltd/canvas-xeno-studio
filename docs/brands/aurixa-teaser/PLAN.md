## The idea

Every property comes with three questions, and each of the films answers one of them:

- **"Is it right for them?"** This is Film 1, which follows 14 Wattle Grove through the whole system.
- **"Are they who they say they are?"** This is Film 2, the AML/CTF Compliance Passport.
- **"Can they afford it?"** This is Film 3, Borrowing Capacity. It's also Film 3's own line.

The teaser asks the three questions over a city at night. 14 Wattle Grove, the films' property, is a gold light in
Sydney's north-west. Each question draws a line of light from it to an office across the city, and a window lights
up there with that film's object: the floor plan, the Passport, the Borrowing Capacity card. The three offices
join into a triangle around the property. The triangle stands up off the map and becomes the Aurixa emblem, which
is itself a triangle, and the property's light becomes its orb. Then come three titles: "Three questions. Three
films. One system."

Why this way:

- **It's new.** None of the films shows the night, the map, the lines or the triangle. The teaser has a world of its
  own, and the series gets a shape it can keep.
- **It's tied to the films, loosely.** It has the same property, the three films' subjects as its three questions
  (each named by its film), their three key objects, and the films' voice, palette, type and logo. Every other frame
  is new.
- **The logo is the answer.** The emblem is a triangle with an orb at its heart. Three questions around one
  property make the same shape, so the brand arrives as the answer to the questions rather than as a sign-off.
- **It claims nothing.** There are three questions, three titles and the brand's own line. It shows no figure and
  makes no promise, and nothing in it goes beyond what the films already show.

## How it looks

The storyboard and the style frames are drawn in code, as the teaser itself would be. They aren't mock-ups, and
production builds on the same renderers ([`scripts/aurixa-teaser/`](../../../scripts/aurixa-teaser/)).

- **The night.** The camera is 900 km up over Bass Strait, looking north-north-east. Melbourne is at the foot of
  the frame, Sydney right of centre and Brisbane toward the curve of the horizon. Every town of 1,000 people or more
  is a spread of light sized by its population (GeoNames). The coast is GLOBE's land mask, and the ground is NASA's
  Blue Marble lit as if by moonlight. A thin line of airglow sits above the limb, as it does in night photographs
  from orbit.
- **The city.** Sydney at night from above. The coast, the harbour and the bays are the real ones (GSHHG's
  full-resolution shoreline), and each suburb's light comes from the people who live there (GeoNames). The streets
  are drawn from those people, not traced from a map, so none of them is a real street and none is named. The map
  is turned so the harbour and the city's heart sit at the top.
- **The offices.** The three lines end at the points of three real suburbs around the property: South Windsor
  (01), Edensor Park (02) and Hornsby (03). Their sides are 28 to 30 km, close to equilateral, with the property
  inside, so the triangle can become the emblem. `offices.py` shows how they were chosen. None is named on screen;
  the offices are the films' professionals, not real businesses.
- **The windows.** Each window is glass with a gold hairline, lit against the night, and holds one film's object:
  14 Wattle Grove's floor plan in gold, the Compliance Passport's cover, and Jordan Ellis's Borrowing Capacity
  card. The product screens carry DEMO WORKSPACE, as in the films. Nothing is pressed and no figure appears.
- **The answer.** As the triangle stands up, the city tips back into perspective and its lights meet the logo's own
  skyline. The triangle's lines settle onto the emblem's edges and dissolve into the supplied art. The emblem itself
  is never drawn: the lines are the questions' lines, and the logo is the supplied file.
- **The type.** The films' faces: Playfair Display, with each line's key phrase in gold italic over a hairline, and
  the gold mono for labels. The teaser drops the films' caption panel and sets its type on the night with a soft
  shade behind it, so it reads as a title sequence rather than as captions.
- **The cover.** Frame 0 is the thumbnail: the night, the gold light and the opening line, which is on screen from
  the first frame.

## The rules it keeps

- **No figures.** Film 3's card shows no capacity: the pointer waits, and nothing is calculated (guardrail 2).
- **The product on its demo record.** Jordan Ellis and 14 Wattle Grove, with DEMO WORKSPACE on the Passport and
  the card (guardrail 8).
- **The Passport, but not its sharing.** It's shown, not passed between businesses, so it says nothing about
  relying on another firm's checks (guardrail 4). "Compliant" appears nowhere.
- **Questions, not claims.** There are no absolutes, and the only statement is the brand's own line (guardrails 3
  and 5).
- **Spoken to the professional.** These are the questions a professional asks about a client (guardrail 6).
- **No AI imagery beyond the supplied logo art.** The night views are drawn from data, and the floor plan is a
  drawing (guardrail 10).
- **The supplied logo**, placed and blended, never redrawn. The lettering is the supplied lettering.
- **Clear of the platforms.** The top 250 px and the bottom 360 px carry nothing that matters, and the lines sit
  between 1300 and 1560 px.

## Decisions

1. **The concept and script as written.** Approve them, or tell me what to change.
2. **The music.** _Recommended:_ the original score. It gives the teaser its own sound, and it needs no licence
   beyond your ElevenLabs plan. The alternative is _National Treasures_, the films' track: it ties the teaser to them
   but makes it sound like them, and its licence is still to confirm.
3. **The voice.** _Recommended:_ the eight lines in Gabby's voice, recorded as for the films. The alternative is no
   voice, with the type and the score carrying it, so it plays the same with the sound off.
4. **The release line.** "THREE FILMS · WATCH NOW" if all three post together, or "FILM 1 · [date]" if they go out
   one a week. Tell me the plan or the words.
5. **The questions' words**, if you'd like them sharper. The alternatives are "Is it the right one?" for Film 1 and
   "Can they prove who they are?" for Film 2. "Can they afford it?" is Film 3's own line, so I'd keep it.
6. **A 15-second cut.** Optional, once this cut is approved. It would run the night (3 s), the three questions (2 s
   each), the triangle becoming the emblem (3 s) and the brand (3 s).

## Before it runs publicly

- **The map's credits**, in the post's description: GeoNames (CC BY 4.0) and the GSHHG shorelines (Wessel and Smith,
  LGPL). NASA's Blue Marble, Natural Earth and NOAA's GLOBE land mask are public domain; crediting them is courteous.
- **ElevenLabs' plan**, which must cover commercial use, for the voice and the score.
- **The compliance review**, if you want the teaser held to the films' review. It shows no figure or claim, but it
  names the Compliance Passport and shows the Borrowing Capacity card.

## How it will be made, once approved

- **The night views** are rendered in code at every step of the camera's path. The orbit falls toward Sydney
  through matched scales, and the city resolves beneath it (`orbit.py`, `city.py`).
- **The lines, windows and type** are drawn in the browser, as the films are. The triangle stands up as a 3D move
  over the city tipped into perspective, and the logo is the supplied art, dissolved in.
- **The voice** is your recording, timed word by word. The score is generated to the plan above and fitted to 35 s,
  and the mix is measured.
- **Two gates** in the studio project _Aurixa Teaser_: the animatic (the whole cut with its sound), then the film.
