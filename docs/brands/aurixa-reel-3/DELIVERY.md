## The picture

Every screen is the product's own, drawn by its code on the demo record: Jordan Ellis, 14 Wattle Grove. Every figure
is one the product's engine computed from that record. Each carries the illustrative label, and every Aurixa screen
carries DEMO WORKSPACE.

The film was drawn in nineteen stretches on twelve machines, eleven of them helper sessions, at final quality: eight
drawings a frame over a 180-degree shutter, at twice the size. Each stretch was checked by agreement. A sample of its
frames was drawn again and put to the vote, and three of its frames were drawn again on this machine.

Two things were drawn again after that, and spliced in only where two separate draws of every frame agreed
(`verify_run.py splice`, each splice recorded in its stretch's `film.json`):

- **The words the brand keeps out of frame**, now under covers of the screen's own ground:
  - on the Borrowing Capacity page as it comes into focus (05.5 and 05.6): "SERVICEABILITY BAND", the Recommendations
    row and the audit's line on serviceability;
  - on the liabilities card (05.4 to 05.6): the car loan's type chip, which printed the engine's key `vehicle_loan`,
    and the credit card's beside it;
  - on the Aurixa Agent's card once the act is done (09.3): "✓ Approved", at the frame's top edge.
- **Five short stretches where one of a machine's three browsers had drawn its frames at a slightly different
  raster scale.** In motion that cannot be seen; where the picture holds still it reads as a shimmer. The stretches
  were in 07.1, 07.4 and 08.1 to 08.3.

## Before it runs publicly

- **A compliance review.** This reel shows modelled borrowing figures: a borrowing capacity, a stress-tested capacity,
  a monthly surplus, a debt-to-income ratio and a purchase power. Each is the product's engine's own figure for a
  hypothetical client, and each sits under the illustrative label. The brand kit leaves open whether ASIC's RG 234 and
  the credit rules reach a software vendor's advertising for borrowing-capacity features. Its process guardrail asks
  for a compliance review of any piece with financial figures before it is published (decision 12).
- **The music's licence.** _National Treasures_ is Drake's commercial recording. The studio records it as owned by the
  brand, and its licence for public use is still to confirm, as for Reels 1 and 2. Before the film is posted, confirm
  that Aurixa holds the right to use it in a promotional video on each platform it goes to. Without that, a platform
  may mute it or take the video down.
- **ElevenLabs' plan.** The voiceover is the client's ElevenLabs voice, so the account's plan must cover commercial
  use, including ads (decision 1).
- **The free trial.** The film offers a 14-day free trial, in the voice and on the closing card, as Reels 1 and 2 do.
  The site's pricing page, in its code as of 13 October, answers "Is there a free trial?" with a sandbox set up on
  request. Confirm that the 14-day trial is the offer when it runs (decision 8, the brand's guardrail 9).
