# Aurixa Reel 3: Borrowing Capacity

How Reel 3 is made from the client's voiceover:

- what the film shows, shot by shot, on the voice's clock and paced to _National Treasures_;
- what each shot is made from, and what is generated;
- where the voiceover and the product part ways, and what the film does there;
- what the client decides before anything is built.

Written 24 October 2026. It builds on [Reel 2's plan](../aurixa-reel-2/PLAN.md), [the plan for Reels 2 and 3](../aurixa-reels-2-and-3/PLAN.md)
and [Reel 1's handover](../aurixa-reel-1/HANDOVER.md), and doesn't repeat their method, rules or checks. The studio keeps
the work as _Aurixa Reel 3_ (`8cf0b42c-6866-499b-8ce6-1035c66a4417`).

## 1. The voiceover

Measured on 24 October 2026:

| Format   | MP3, 128 kbit/s, mono, 44.1 kHz. Its tag names the same FFmpeg-based encoder as Reel 2's (`Lavf60.16.101`)                              |
| Length   | 129.567 s; the speech ends at 129.52 s                                                                                                  |
| Loudness | -20.9 LUFS integrated, a loudness range of 4.1 LU, true peak -1.3 dBFS. The mix brings it to -14 LUFS                                   |
| Words    | 264, in 25 sentences, with 36 pauses of 0.18 s or more                                                                                  |

**It is Reel 1's voice.** Speaker embeddings were taken over 10-second windows (WeSpeaker ResNet34, trained on
VoxCeleb). Windows from this recording match Reel 1's master as closely as windows from one recording match each other:

- Reel 3 against Reel 1's master: 0.913 on average (0.854 to 0.954);
- within Reel 1: 0.916;
- within Reel 3: 0.917.

Against Reel 2's recording it scores 0.912. The file is dated 13 October 2026, like Reels 1 and 2's.

**Two recognisers read it, and they agree.**

- A recogniser run on this machine placed every word: NVIDIA's Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2, in sherpa-onnx.
- A second, Whisper small.en, read every sentence again (`second_opinion.py`).

The two readings are word for word the same. They differ only in each recogniser's spelling of "Aurixa" (ARIXA, Erixa,
Orixo) and a comma or two. That includes the two lines in section 2 that read oddly.

**A question of quality.** As with Reel 2, this copy is an MP3 that has been through an encoder at least once. If
ElevenLabs' own download of this voiceover is still in the account, use that instead. Otherwise this file will do.

**The word times are provisional.** Each word's end is clipped to the pause measured in the audio
(`voiceover-words.json`). The studio's own preparation replaces these times once the recording is imported
(`import_audio`, as a voiceover), which asks the client to confirm the voice's consent (decision 1).

## 2. The script, as the captions

`scripts/aurixa-reel-3/shot_times.py` checks those words against the voice, shot by shot, so a caption can't say
anything the voice doesn't.

**Two lines to confirm.** Both recognisers hear each of them the same way:

  obtain an indicative borrowing capacity assessment".

  use.

The captions follow the voice. If there is a written script, send it: the captions then follow its punctuation, but
still say only what is spoken.

## 3. The story

Reel 1 followed 14 Wattle Grove from an enquiry to a recorded decision. Reel 2 told the same purchase from the
compliance side. Reel 3 tells it from the finance side: can the client afford it, and how does the answer reach the
broker?

1. **The question** (light, unbranded). The property is found, and the question lands: can they afford it? The old
   answer is documents chased, spreadsheets juggled and people waited on, until the frame cools and the music falls
   silent.
2. **Everything changes** (the brand arrives). The logo leaps out on the drop.
3. **One questionnaire.** The client's fact find, filled in once, is imported in one go and becomes Jordan Ellis' record
   and client card.
4. **One click.** The music falls away and the pointer waits over Calculate Capacity. On the drop the assessment
   resolves, and the page names what the voice names, one at a time: the capacity, the deposit, the commitments and
   the basis.
5. **The chosen property.** 14 Wattle Grove comes into the scenario workspace. The deposit is set, the structure tried
   and scenarios saved, and the Borrowing Capacity Snapshot is drawn as charts.
6. **The handover.** One save makes the chosen scenario the current assessment. It reaches Jamie Chen at Harbour Finance
   Group, the broker allocated to the client, and Jamie opens the same figures in the Finance Portal.
7. **What each gains.** The client's report in their own portal, the broker's context, and the deal moving on to its
   next stage.
8. **Just the beginning.** The rest of the platform docks into the Command Centre, and the Aurixa Agent proposes an act
   that the professional approves.
9. **The close** (shared with Reels 1 and 2). What it means, the brand arriving, the living call to action, three
   statements and the closing card.

Every Aurixa screen carries the Demo workspace label, and every value on it comes from the brand kit's demo record
(section 5.3).

- it sits in the pause before the shot's first word, 0.15 s ahead of the speech;

- either way, it falls on the 30 fps grid.

The film clock is the voice's clock held open in its silences to land on _National Treasures_ (section 7,

**How it was solved:**

- **The holds.** 32 holds, 15.3 s in all, every one inside a measured silence. The longest:
  - 2.0 s after "just the beginning.", in the breakdown;
  - 1.1 s on the agent's card;
  - 0.9 s before the brand's drop;
  - about 0.6 s each before the click, before "Aurixa Systems has arrived" and before the statements.
- **The cuts.** 13 land on a bar line, 10 on a beat and 3 within a frame of one. The other 8 sit inside phrases that no
  silence can move. At the animatic each either moves to the nearest beat, up to a quarter of a second ahead of its
  words, or stays on its word.
- **The anchors.** Every one lands on its bar: both drops, the breakdown, the stop, the biggest section, the three
  statements, the closing card, "the operating system", "Start your free trial today." and the press.

**The joins.**

- Every edit point sits 20 ms ahead of its bar's attack. Every clear attack where the fit cuts the track is within
  18.4 ms of the grid, so no note is cut.
- Each join is the studio's 30 ms crossfade. The exception is where a piece comes in out of quiet onto a bar the track
  is already sounding: the build after the stop, each statement's hit, and the closing card's return. There it enters
  on its bar line (`enter`), as Reel 2 learned. The music-at-cuts check measures every hit on its cut in every render.

**The mix.** Where the track does what it did in Reels 1 and 2, it sits at the levels the client approved there:

- the intro at 0.6, ducking to 0.25;
- the groove at 0.5, ducking to 0.22;
- the breakdown never ducked.

As in Reel 2, the voice says "experience" and "decision" softly: 6.0 and 9.5 dB below its average in the speech band.
So each statement meets its hit on its first word, "your", and the last hit is the quietest. The studio's mixer brings
the film to -14 LUFS with a true peak of -1 dBTP or lower. Its mix report gives the voice's margin over the music in
every section, and the voice is kept at least 8 dB clear of it.

**Length.** The solve gives 2:27.2, inside three minutes for Instagram, Facebook and YouTube Shorts, and well inside
TikTok's and LinkedIn's limits. It is 7.2 s over X's 140 s for a free account. The track's bars leave little to trim,
and neither alternative is recommended:

- **The shortest fit.** The intro and the stall can't lose a bar: a cut would then land before its own words.
  - One bar less of breakdown and a second less of ring is the most that can go, at 2:24.3 (solved).
  - That leaves 0.13 s after "just the beginning.", and the film is still over 140 s.
- **A cut-down** for X would drop whole phrases.

**Recommended:** keep the room to breathe, and post to X from a Premium account, which allows longer uploads.

## 10. Found in the product along the way

These are the product's own, outside this reel. They are recorded here for the product's team.

1. **The target verdict is never "achievable."**
   - In `computeAcquisitionCapacity` (`supabase/functions/_shared/scenarioDeltaEngine.ts`), the loan required is the
     target less the cash. The settlement then spends the same cash on the deposit, so "net cash after settlement" is
     always minus the duty, costs and deducted LMI, wherever the LVR cap doesn't bind.
   - So `meetsTarget` is false for every target, and the panel reads "Short by $0" in red.
   - The tests that use it state `meetsTarget` in their fixtures rather than compute it.
   - The fix: count the duty, the costs and any deducted LMI in the loan required.
2. **The Client Portal's dashboard loads the client's borrowing capacity and never draws it**
   (`pages/portal/PortalDashboard.tsx`, line 58).
3. **Compliance words in two panels.** "Borrowing capacity assessments are calculated using APRA-compliant
   serviceability rules." (the Finance Portal's panel) and "for APRA compliance" (the Results panel). The brand's
   guardrails keep "compliant" out of its content, and a product screen is content once it is filmed.
4. **A cut address.** A rented home's address is cut short in the commitments twice:
   - at 30 characters by the engine ("Rent Expense (7/22 Banksia Parade, Banksia R...)");
   - at 20 by the Borrowing Capacity page.

   The engine once cut a property's address the same way in its income table.

5. **The fact find workbook** reads "Replace this area with your logo in Orixa" on its White Label Setup sheet.
6. **Two spellings.** "Command Center" (Team & Partners) and "Command Centre" (elsewhere); "Modelling" (the tab) and
   "Modeling" (a lever).
7. **A payoff counted twice.** Cash on hand that the Capital Flow Canvas routes to paying out a debt is still counted
   as purchase cash. In the car loan scenario, $18,000 is counted twice in its effective purchase power. Recorded for
   the product's team and not built, because it changes the engine's figures.
8. **The Snapshot's date is UTC.** A Snapshot exported at 9:13 am AEDT on 24 October 2026 is dated "23 October 2026"
   on its cover. The film keeps that row out of frame.
9. **A capacity with a sign.** Portfolio Scenario Modelling prints the scenario's borrowing capacity as "+$964,993",
   formatted as a change, beside its own change against the base.
10. **A colour in a title.** The Client Portal lists the report as "Borrowing Capacity – $1,111,628 (green)": the
    band's colour, not its name.
11. **Two counts of one access.** Team & Partners shows Jamie Chen with "11/12 sections"; the Finance Portal says
    "11 of 13 sections accessible".
12. **A type key on the page.** The Borrowing Capacity page's liabilities print each one's type beside its name. The
